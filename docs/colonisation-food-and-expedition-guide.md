# Food and expedition preparation

Status: guidance for the existing provisional Colonisation & Expansion playtest. This adds no balance or save changes.

Open **Worlds**, choose an owned world, and compare its weekly food output with resident demand. The local balance is before imports and exports. Stored food is finite. Deliveries use actual surplus, routes and capacity; they are not a guaranteed reserve. Shortage weeks count consecutive weeks when resident food demand was not fully met.

In the starting scenario, Earth has 1,000 residents, 20 stored food and output 14 against demand 10. Mars has 250 residents, 4 stored food and output 0 against demand 3. Earth can provide a 100-person source cohort; Mars cannot meet the existing source-population safeguards. Choose your settler source separately from your stronger industrial yard.

On a food-deficit yard, open **Planetary buildings** and consider **Hydroponic extension** before a long ship order. It costs 40 Industry and 1 Dilithium and adds base Food 6 with power demand 1. Construction consumes real work and local materials gradually. The extra output begins after actual completion; no delivery or completion date is promised. **Fabrication annex** adds base Industry 6 but costs 80 Industry and 2 Dilithium with power demand 2; it does not supply food. Base building effects are not guaranteed effective output under current power, politics or finite deposits.

Build a transport at an actual shipyard and a settlement kit at the source world. Kits cannot be transferred by an invented remote action: bring the empty transport to the world holding the people, food and kit. In **Transports**, choose the actual cohort and review loading 100 settlers, one local kit and 8-20 food. At least 500 people must remain, and no more than a quarter of the selected cohort may embark. Keep enough food for the residents as well as the voyage.

Use normal navigation and planetary survey, then review deployment at a suitable observed world. A founding camp needs at least four fed weeks and actual establishment work. An empty reusable transport can return, load real food/materials without passengers and deliver supplies. No food purchase, free stock, automatic safety or kit teleport is implied. A first-week local population reduction is not by itself evidence of starvation; ordinary migration also changes population.
