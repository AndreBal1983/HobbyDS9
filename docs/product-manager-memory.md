## Agreed design direction

[The design index](design/README.md) governs the migrated decisions. Preserve its agreed/proposed/open labels.

- **AI:** persistent victory goals and coordinated operational plans; faction abilities, personality and difficulty are independent data profiles. Five fair difficulties improve planning/search without resource cheats or hidden information. This is intended direction; the current simple AI is not the full planner. See [strategic AI](design/strategic-ai.md).
- **Ship design:** shared internal capacity, compatibility/mount constraints, meaningful power allocations and Engineering-developed hull/component versions. Hull investments should support multiple roles; mission, timing, affordability and logistics should inform designs. Detailed balance, refits, AI design and the concurrent Engineering portfolio require scoped work. See [ship design](design/ship-design.md).
- **Time and mobilisation:** one week per strategic turn; sector movement is separate from warp factors. Credits may expedite eligible work within physical/social constraints. Sensor investment creates warning time. Prices, speeds, timing and calendar integration need explicit implementation and acceptance. See [time and movement](design/time-movement-mobilisation.md).
- **Exploration:** surveys, first contact and procedural missions should connect to shared campaign consequences, including expedition-repair dilemmas. The complete mission/survey/diplomacy infrastructure is future scope. See [exploration and missions](design/exploration-missions.md).
- **Combat:** no hard fleet-size cap; command adds positive coordination capability. Detection, tracking and interception are distinct. Intended combat uses abstract formation-relative geometry, simultaneous round orders, persistent orders and objectives beyond destruction. Unity visualizes authoritative results. See [tactical combat](design/tactical-combat.md).
- **Tactical terminology:** Trek-style pattern callouts are proposed presentation aliases for practical maneuver orders, not new buffs or canon claims. Individual subsystem targeting is explicitly parked and undecided, not an approved future commitment.

## Game design interview process

Adopted by the owner on 13 October 2026: use the principles of [Matt Pocock's grill-me](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/tree/main/skills/productivity/grill-me) and its [grilling instructions](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/productivity/grilling/SKILL.md) to govern substantial design discussions in this product-manager chat. This is an adapted working practice; the upstream skill/plugin is not installed.

Start from the player's intended experience and the concrete decision being designed. Read existing decisions and relevant implementation contracts first. Research discoverable facts directly; ask the owner for preferences, trade-offs and intent. Preserve settled decisions unless new evidence or the owner explicitly reopens them.

Map the design as dependent decisions. Ask only questions whose prerequisites are settled. Use one focused question, or at most three independent questions, per round. Give a recommended answer and its reason, plus meaningful alternatives and consequences. Mark recommendations as proposals; a lack of reply is not agreement. Wait for answers before resolving dependent design choices, while continuing useful independent research.

Pressure-test the selected design with concrete campaign examples: the normal player action, a resource-constrained case, an exploit or dominant strategy, and a failure/interruption where applicable. Check how the AI uses the same rules and what information the player needs to make the choice. Examine interactions with economy, research, Engineering, movement and combat only where relevant to the scoped feature. Identify determinism, persistence and compatibility consequences before assigning implementation.

Conclude each discussion with a concise decision record: player outcome, agreed rules, proposed tuning, open questions, explicit exclusions, and acceptance examples. Keep decisions in the existing design chapter and implementation/acceptance requirements in the applicable milestone document. Record rationale and rejected alternatives when they explain a meaningful trade-off; avoid duplicating specifications in this handover or creating a competing glossary.

Stop interviewing when the scoped milestone has clear behavior and acceptance criteria. Explicitly defer unrelated branches of the wider game instead of trying to settle the entire roadmap. Read back material decisions for correction; ask for an answer only where a consequential product choice remains unresolved. Existing authorization remains valid, and routine implementation choices do not require a new approval ritual.

Implementation prompts derived from the discussion should specify the verified starting checkpoint, intended player behavior, included and excluded scope, affected boundaries, compatibility decisions and required acceptance evidence. New ideas raised during implementation return to the design discussion if they materially change that contract.
