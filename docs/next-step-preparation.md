## Product decisions agreed in this chat

The owner confirmed all three decisions on 13 October 2026:

1. Prove the first playable slice in a separate opt-in scenario with explicitly authored v2 ships. Existing designer-built v1 ships stay on their supported path. Modular v2 blueprint compilation is later scope.
2. Persist ammunition and directional shield damage. No automatic ammunition refill or between-battle shield recovery in this initial scenario. Existing v2 in-battle recharge remains. Physical resupply and recovery are later milestones; this constraint is not universal final campaign balance.
3. Prove destruction/basic battles and the existing maximum of three rounds first. Strategic retreat, Breakthrough and other objective exits are disabled pending lawful map consequences. No teleport, conquest reward or implied objective completion is added.

These narrow the migration proposal; they do not approve numerical equipment balance, formation formulas or a new campaign default. Hull damage remains persistent. Do not silently add healing through load, encounter creation, profile selection or UI refresh.

Additional agreement, 13 October 2026: strategic fleets enter combat as distinct tactical command units. In the first slice each participating fleet maps to one formation; fleets following the same route remain separate, and combat does not automatically split a fleet into role groups. This decision is recorded in the [tactical design chapter](design/tactical-combat.md). It does not define interception/encounter eligibility or capability aggregation. The fleet-to-formation identity question in the lifecycle register below is now settled; formation statistics, initial geometry and mixed-source routing remain open.

Further agreement: keep one simple set of fleet-level orders and let ships contribute automatically from their actual equipment/capabilities. Individual tactical mobility remains meaningful within a fleet; a universal slowest-ship tactical-speed rule was not adopted. Composition benefits arise through tactical resolution with costs and limits, not class-label buffs. Existing interception opportunities support part of this direction; automatic mobility/role contributions and aggregation still need design and may require a separately scoped resolver change. Reconcile that gap before claiming the first lifecycle/UI slice expresses the complete combined-arms design.

Post-battle agreement, clarified by the owner: nondecisive three-round survivors retain their stocks/damage and sector, then choose strategic actions such as leaving, re-engaging or following. Pursuit is ordinary strategic-map movement; catching requires reaching the same sector at the same time with lawful hostile contact/engagement intent. Both leaving use lawful routes without mutual pursuit. The earlier abstract pursuit-versus-escape contest and proposed departure-sector check were rejected; do not add a separate chase roll or remote route-interception resolver. No immediate same-turn battle loop, teleport or replenishment. See [tactical combat](design/tactical-combat.md). Simultaneous/intermediate-sector travel timing, weighted-edge occupancy, contact following and multi-fleet policies remain open. This adds a strategic movement/contact dependency; within-battle strategic exit objectives remain deferred.

Movement-stop agreement: meeting and engaging stops participating fleets' remaining movement for the turn in the encounter sector. Survivors require fresh strategic orders for the following turn; interrupted routes must not automatically resume. Other fleets are unaffected. Finalization neither advances the clock again nor grants same-turn movement. Movement continuation after battle is now settled; precise shared travel timing and weighted-edge contact remain open. This is intended behavior requiring scoped Simulation/movement acceptance, not a claim that the current end-of-Movement encounter allocator implements it.

Mutual re-engagement is explicitly confirmed: hostile survivors that both remain and choose combat receive a new encounter during the following turn's resolution, with actual retained ships/damage/shields/ammunition. No fourth round, automatic same-turn repeat or encounter-entry replenishment. Acceptance must exercise consecutive battles with depleted stocks.

Starting distance is now agreed for the first playable slice: every new battle initializes opposing formation pairs at Distant range. Range can change through normal tactical maneuver resolution afterward. Variable approach/ambush starts are deferred; this decision does not set facings or refill ship stocks.

Engagement visibility/cloak direction: uncloaked ships are visible in scanning range, but upcoming orders stay hidden. **Free attack is now defined:** a fully cloaked side fires its ordinary round-one attack against visible opponents without targeted return fire; there is no extra opening volley or round. Normal power, cadence, firing opportunities and ammunition costs apply. Mixed fleets' cloaked ships have the same round-one target protection while visible ships fight normally. All surviving cloaked ships uncloak after round one regardless of firing and stay uncloaked/ordinarily targetable in later rounds. Mutual cloaks, next-encounter cloak initialization, incoming-ordnance defense, scan detail and counter-detection remain open. This requires new explicitly scoped cloak equipment/state, observation and resolver support absent from PR #19; decide milestone placement before commissioning it. The previously suggested fully known equipment test scenario was not approved by the owner.

Reactive defense is now agreed: torpedoes from hidden cloaked attackers may be intercepted using actual powered equipment and available firing opportunities. No separate player command, guaranteed interception or free capacity. An engagement without visible opponents should communicate caution using lawful information, without revealing hidden ships or changing standing orders automatically. Incoming-ordnance defense is therefore settled; mutual cloak, next-encounter initialization and counter-detection remain open.

Strategic cloak initialization is now agreed: one fleet setting causes cloak-capable ships to approach each encounter cloaked; other ships stay visible. The setting persists across battles, but all ships uncloak after round one and cannot re-cloak later in the same encounter. It does not restore stocks. Capability must come from authoritative equipment, not names or factions. Mutual cloak handling, operational cloak constraints/costs and counter-detection remain open; the next-encounter initialization question is settled.

Mutual cloak handling is now agreed: two fully cloaked opposing sides have no visible offensive targets in round one, so they maneuver without offensive fire, then both uncloak for round two. The first round still counts toward the three-round limit; no additional volley or compensating round. Do not spend ammunition on a shot that did not occur. Core cloak/visibility behavior is settled; operational costs/restrictions and counter-detection are still future design questions, not implied equipment/resolver support.

Scan detail is now agreed: visible enemy ship types, hull condition and shield strength are shown; enemy ammunition reserves and upcoming orders remain hidden. Apply the same filtering to round reports/replay/tooltips, not only the planner. Observed launches may be reported without exposing internal remaining stock. Exact display precision and broader equipment disclosure remain proposed; this does not approve the earlier omniscient test-scenario suggestion.

Fleet-local screening is now agreed: fleets do not mingle, and escorts protect only ships in their own fleet. Transports/colony ships must share that fleet to receive its screening. No optional allied-fleet protection picker or cross-fleet defensive contribution in the campaign slice. This supersedes the UX specification's proposed allied protection selection and separate Escort/Transport formation examples. Restrict the existing standalone designated-allied-formation capability at the authoritative campaign boundary; do not merely hide the control or silently change standalone goldens.

Targeting control is now agreed: choose an enemy fleet focus, with suitable ship targets selected automatically within it. No individual-ship firing targets, subsystem controls or per-ship fire management. Visible ship inspection is still allowed. Focus cannot bypass lawful target/fire access. Reconcile the UX specification's ship-priority dropdown with this simpler interface; automatic policy and unavailable-focus fallback still need a deterministic backend definition.

Focus fallback is now agreed: use the chosen fleet where legal targets exist; otherwise automatically use another visible hostile fleet with valid targets for the firing opportunity. Explain fallback in the report, preserve standing focus and respect all normal access/resource constraints. The policy is settled; deterministic ranking and within-fleet selection still require scoped backend rules.
